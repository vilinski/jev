Reading the results

Speed. Jev is the fastest and steadiest (p50 480 ms, p95 554 ms). Clef has similar typical speed but a long tail (p95 1303 ms, one call 4.3 s). OpenAI is the slowest typically (719 ms) but has no tail beyond 1.3 s.

Accuracy. All three are close (overall 93% Jev, 96% Clef, 96% OpenAI) and the differences come from a handful of items, see the list below. Jev is flawless on yes/no and routing but weakest on severity; Clef is best on severity; OpenAI got one routing answer wrong in one of three runs, the only decision flip of the whole benchmark.

Calibration is where they differ. OpenAI’s log-probabilities are sharp: it is the best on yes/no (Brier 0.0014) but its confidence is 0.98 when wrong versus 0.97 when right, so confidence does not flag its mistakes. Jev is lower when wrong (0.74 vs 0.96) and flags every vague input as uncertain, which OpenAI and Clef do on only 25% of them. Clef has the weakest Brier scores.

Cost. Not measured in money. Jev and Clef answer all questions of a request in one call. OpenAI makes one request per question, so a batch of N questions costs N requests (fast in parallel, not cheaper).

Caveats. Synthetic, small (60 items) and easy: yes/no and routing sit near the ceiling, and a single item changes a percentage by 2 points. Only 3 repeats. Latency is from the jev CLI with a fresh TLS connection per call, at different times of day for OpenAI and the others (Jev and Clef ran together earlier). The OpenAI result depends on the prompt that jev writes for it and on the model (gpt-4.1-mini); other models would score differently. Severity labels are my own judgement.
